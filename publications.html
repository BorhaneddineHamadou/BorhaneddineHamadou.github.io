Under Review

Borhaneddine Hamadou, Chokri Mraidha, Nikolaos Matragkas, "Stochasticity-Aware Test Reduction and Prioritization for Autonomous Driving Systems", under double-blind review at ICSE 2027.

ADS test verdicts are not deterministic — flaky regressions surface in only a fraction of runs. SA-TRP casts test selection as budget allocation over repeated executions with Beta–Bernoulli posteriors and Thompson sampling, improving regression-detection F1 by 34–62% on openpilot and Autoware commit histories.

Borhaneddine Hamadou, Chokri Mraidha, Nikolaos Matragkas, "PWCC: Probability-Weighted Combinatorial Coverage for Risk-Informed Scenario Testing of Autonomous Systems", ASE'2026 — IEEE/ACM International Conference on Automated Software Engineering (Research Track), to appear. [pdf]

A coverage metric that answers "have we tested enough?" — it weights combinatorial coverage by a learned model of real-world operating conditions and declares adequacy against a risk-calibrated threshold. Strongest predictor of fault detection across four autonomous driving datasets and seven baselines.

Borhaneddine Hamadou, Chokri Mraidha, Nikolaos Matragkas, "BayScen: Standards-Grounded Scenario Generation for Autonomous Vehicle Testing via Bayesian Networks and Capability Abstraction", ISSRE'2026 — IEEE International Symposium on Software Reliability Engineering (Research Track), to appear. [pdf]

Resolves the coverage–criticality–realism trilemma of scenario generation: an ISO 34503-grounded capability abstraction makes combinatorial coverage tractable, while a Bayesian network learned from real-world data keeps scenarios physically plausible. Critical scenarios are ×7 more likely to be physically plausible than adversarial search, with the highest ODD coverage.

Borhaneddine Hamadou, "Danger Is a Counterfactual: Toward a Falsifiable Continuous Test Oracle for Autonomous Driving Systems", ASE'2026 — IEEE/ACM International Conference on Automated Software Engineering (Student Research Competition, Graduate), Munich, Germany, to appear. [pdf]

A simulated driving test is decided by a single bit — collision or not — which calls a four-centimetre miss at highway speed a pass. This work measures harm instead of scoring it: the fraction of a run's plausible neighbouring worlds that end in a crash, weighted by severity, making a danger estimate falsifiable for the first time. One openpilot run every telemetry baseline passes turns out to hold a third of the whole suite's harm.
