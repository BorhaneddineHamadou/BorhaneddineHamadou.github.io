About Me

I am a PhD researcher at CEA List (Université Paris-Saclay), working on scenario-based testing and safety validation of autonomous vehicle systems. My research develops principled methods — combining Bayesian networks, combinatorial coverage, and statistical modeling — to answer the questions that matter for deploying autonomous systems safely: which scenarios should we test, how do we know we have tested enough, and how do we catch regressions that only appear intermittently?

My work is grounded in industry safety standards (ISO 21448 / SOTIF, ISO 34503) and evaluated on real autonomous driving stacks (Autoware, openpilot) in simulation (CARLA, MetaDrive). It has led to publications at ISSRE and ASE, two of the top venues in software reliability and automated software engineering.

Before my PhD, I earned a Master of Engineering in Computer Systems Engineering from ESI Algiers (the first computer science school in Algeria), and spent a year at LAAS-CNRS (Toulouse) building machine-learning models to predict Quality-of-Service degradation for autonomous vehicles in edge-computing environments.

I am actively seeking industrial R&D, research-scientist, and applied-research positions in companies working on autonomous and safety-critical systems.

Research Interests

  • Probabilistic modeling & Bayesian networks
  • Machine learning & time-series forecasting
